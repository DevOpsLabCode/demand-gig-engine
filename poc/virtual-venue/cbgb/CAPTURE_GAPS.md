# CBGB reconstruction capture gaps

The web/archive evidence is enough to start a historical POC, but not enough to claim a metrically exact reconstruction. These are the highest-value gaps to close next.

## Priority 1 — current 315 Bowery survey

The National Register documentation says the post-CBGB commercial tenant largely preserved the club interior. A permission-based current capture is therefore the best way to recover the base geometry.

Capture if permission is granted:

- 360-degree or normal video walk from entrance to rear wall and back.
- Slow perimeter pass at eye height.
- Second pass around 1.0-1.2 m camera height.
- Front facade from straight-on and ±30/45 degree angles.
- Stage area from front, left, right and rear-facing positions.
- Historic bar area and both ends of the bar.
- Ceiling, floor transitions and columns/structural offsets.
- Doorways, stairs and bathroom access.
- A measuring reference in several frames (known-length pole, AprilTag board, or laser measurement).

Do not record private/residential/service areas without explicit permission. Blur bystanders and personal information before publishing a dataset.

## Priority 2 — dimensions and plans

Find and verify:

- Historic ground-floor plan for 313-315 Bowery.
- Storefront width.
- Entrance width and offset from the lot line.
- Interior clear width at front/middle/rear.
- Front-to-stage and stage-to-rear distances.
- Stage width/depth/height.
- Ceiling height and changes in ceiling level.
- Bar length and bar-to-wall distance.
- Bathroom/stair geometry.

Likely research targets include NYC Department of Buildings records, historic alteration applications, the 2006 Municipal Art Society/Place Matters documentation cited by the National Register report, and any drawings retained by the current property/tenant.

## Priority 3 — historical-state evidence

For each period, collect evidence independently rather than mixing decades:

- 1973-1976: earliest CBGB / Television / Patti Smith / Ramones period.
- 1977-1982: punk/no-wave peak.
- 1980s hardcore period.
- 1990s-2006 late club state.
- October 2006 closing state.
- 2008-2009 preserved-interior retail state.

For every item record creator, approximate camera position, date, rights owner/license, visible architectural features and confidence.

## Reference-only hunting list

Search these aggressively, but store only metadata/links until rights are cleared:

- NYPL Digital Collections and manuscript collections.
- Cornell Punk Flyers and related collections.
- NYU Fales Downtown Collection / punk ephemera.
- Museum of the City of New York punk collections.
- Photographer archives: Roberta Bayley, GODLIS, Bob Gruen, Chris Stein and other documented CBGB photographers.
- Newspapers, magazines and documentary footage.
- YouTube/Vimeo/social uploads showing room layout.
- Former employee, performer and audience photo/video collections.
- Closing-night photographs and video.

## Reconstruction confidence rule

Every wall/door/stage/object in the twin should carry a confidence value and evidence links. Suggested bands:

- 0.90-1.00: measured/current capture or multiple consistent primary sources.
- 0.75-0.89: strong multi-source historical reconstruction.
- 0.50-0.74: plausible reconstruction with unresolved measurements.
- below 0.50: visual placeholder only; visibly label it as estimated in research/debug mode.

The public experience should say **Historical Reconstruction** until the geometry and rights record justify a stronger accuracy claim.
