# CBGB Digital Footprint Research Plan

## Purpose

This document defines the deep-research layer for the CBGB historical digital-twin POC. The goal is not to find a handful of famous photographs. The goal is to build a repeatable **Reality Evidence Graph** containing every useful digital footprint we can discover about CBGB, 313-315 Bowery, the Palace Bar/Hotel lineage, the surrounding Bowery streetscape, performances, room configuration, fixtures, stage, audience areas and surviving post-closure fabric.

"Collect everything" means **collect metadata and observations for every discoverable lead**. It does not mean copying every publicly viewable copyrighted image or video. Media enters reconstruction only when the rights record allows the intended derivative/commercial use.

## Research questions

The POC must eventually answer these separately:

1. What was the exact ground-floor CBGB tenant footprint?
2. Which walls/doorways/columns/ceiling features survived from the Palace Bar era?
3. Where exactly were the entrance, historic bar, stage, bathrooms, stairs, backstage and adjacent CB's Gallery spaces?
4. Which features were stable from 1973 through 2006 and which changed?
5. What did the facade/storefront/signage look like for each historical period?
6. Which photographs/videos can legally supply reconstruction pixels versus only geometry observations?
7. Which performances have enough surviving metadata/media to anchor a historical event inside the twin?
8. What survives at 315 Bowery today that can be permissioned and measured?
9. Can repeated archival camera viewpoints solve historic geometry even when the video itself cannot be redistributed?
10. Can we build a time slider whose state is evidence-backed rather than a blended fantasy room?

## Evidence lanes

### Lane A — geometry truth

Highest-value sources:

- permissioned current LiDAR/photogrammetry/360 capture of 315 Bowery;
- architectural plans, DOB alteration drawings and surveys;
- National Register / preservation documentation;
- fire insurance/Sanborn maps and historical building plans;
- measured facade/parcel geometry;
- repeated fixed-feature observations from many independent images/videos.

Geometry observations must have time validity. A door seen in 2009 cannot automatically be asserted for 1975.

### Lane B — reusable visual reconstruction inputs

Preferred order:

1. project-owned capture;
2. explicitly licensed partner archive;
3. public-domain material;
4. CC BY material;
5. CC BY-SA/share-alike material only in an isolated derivation path with obligations reviewed.

Every image/frame must retain original creator, source URL, capture date, license, attribution string and transformations made.

### Lane C — reference-only visual evidence

Examples include NYU Fales footage, Cornell flyers, NYPL copyrighted photographs, photographer archives, documentary footage, press images, social uploads and YouTube/Vimeo material.

Permitted POC use without extra rights is limited to metadata and human-created observations consistent with access terms: e.g. "stage corner visible to left of fixed ceiling fixture in May 1978 reel 305.0006." Do not silently feed the media itself into commercial model training or texture extraction.

### Lane D — event/history metadata

Collect:

- date/time;
- performers and billing order;
- event title;
- source provenance;
- flyer/catalog identifiers;
- known recordings;
- known photographers/videographers;
- setlist where corroborated;
- historical period/state;
- reported venue address (315/317 variants retained exactly as source observations);
- confidence and conflicts.

This becomes the historical-event layer displayed inside the room.

### Lane E — oral history and memories

Interviews, books, former employees, musicians, audience members, roadies and photographers can identify layout and operational details that images miss. They are hypotheses until independently corroborated or measured.

## High-value findings already confirmed

### National Register / preservation record

The Bowery Historic District report establishes the best current historical backbone. It describes 313-315 Bowery as two 1878 tenements later joined/altered in 1934, documents the Palace Bar and Hilly Kristal chronology, says Kristal retained the old bar and numerous fixtures, and states that the CBGB interior was largely preserved after closure.

The report cites a potentially crucial unpublished source: **Lisa Kersavage and Marci Reaven, Historical documentation of 313-315 Bowery, July 24, 2006**, an unpublished letter report to the Landmarks Preservation Commission from Municipal Art Society / Place Matters. Acquiring a copy is a Priority-0 research task.

### NYC parcel/building identity

NYC Department of Buildings data identifies the property as:

- BBL: `1004570005`
- BIN: `1006536`
- address: `313 BOWERY`

Modern secondary property records report approximately 8,168 sq ft lot area, roughly 50.92×166 ft lot dimensions and about 51×162 ft overall building dimensions. These are useful shell checks, but they are not yet a surveyed historic CBGB tenant footprint.

An Acadia Realty brochure reports approximately 14,700 sq ft GLA and 40 ft Bowery frontage for its retail property. That is lease/retail marketing data and must not be conflated with the whole parcel or the exact CBGB room.

### Current-survival opportunity

John Varvatos currently lists its Bowery location at 315 Bowery. Because preservation documentation says much of the former club fabric survived the post-closure conversion, permission for a current non-invasive capture may be the single highest-value geometry acquisition available.

### Wikimedia Commons open seed corpus

Commons currently exposes a 12-image 2009 interior category and a broader 23-file CBGB category. Useful open-license examples include:

- Angie Garrett's 2009 interior and display-stage images — CC BY 2.0;
- `CBGB toilet.jpg` — CC BY 2.0;
- `RamonesCBGB.jpg` — CC BY 2.0 with geolocation/camera information;
- `CBGB Front Facade.jpg` — CC BY-SA 2.5;
- `CBGB club facade.jpg` — CC BY-SA 3.0/GFDL;
- `Metropolis Video at CBGB's 1975.jpg` — CC BY-SA 3.0, especially valuable for early-room evidence;
- `ExCBGB.jpg` — public domain;
- the CBGB awning at the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame — open-license photograph, useful as an artifact-survey lead.

Do not infer a category-wide license. The collector validates every file individually.

### NYU Fales NIGHTCLUBBING archive

This is one of the highest-value historical-footprint discoveries. The Pat Ivers / Emily Armstrong NIGHTCLUBBING archive spans 1970-2009 (bulk 1975-1980) and includes 161 U-matic tapes, 24 Betacam tapes, 26 half-inch video reels and 21 MiniDV items.

The finding aid enumerates substantial CBGB footage, including:

- Dead Boys — October 1977;
- numerous May 1978 Blitz Benefit reels and edits;
- backstage ambience and interviews from the Blitz Benefit;
- a women's-room ambience segment useful for non-stage spatial evidence;
- Cuban Heels and Stiletto Fads — April 1979;
- Voidoids — August 1979;
- Destroy All Monsters, Only Ones and Revelons — September 1979;
- Levi and the Rockats — October 1979;
- Bad Brains — December 1979;
- Pin-ups and Student Teachers — February 1980;
- many other downtown-club recordings that can establish contemporaneous production practices and scene context.

NYU explicitly states that copyright/publicity/privacy rights were not transferred. These records are **D/reference-only until permission is obtained**. The POC should request research access and eventually pursue licensing of selected reels, not copy them merely because access copies exist.

### Cornell Punk Flyers

Cornell's Punk Flyers collection provides event chronology and exact historic wording. Confirmed CBGB examples include a May 5, 1974 Television / The Stilettos flyer at 315 Bowery and later records cataloged with 317 Bowery. The address variants are themselves evidence and must be preserved rather than normalized away until the expanded-space history is solved.

### Photographer archives

GODLIS, Roberta Bayley, Bob Gruen, Chris Stein and other photographers repeatedly documented CBGB. These are high-value licensing targets because a small, carefully licensed set with overlapping viewpoints could materially improve the reconstruction. Do not scrape their copyrighted work into training data.

### Music/event graph sources

MusicBrainz has a CBGB place entity at coordinates approximately `40.725278,-73.991944`, opened 1973 and closed 2006-10-15. Its place/event relationships are suitable as a machine-readable discovery source, subject to the exact MusicBrainz dataset license.

Setlist.fm, Discogs, artist sites, fan databases and user-edited histories can add recall but are lower-confidence and require primary/archival corroboration for important claims.

## Time-state model

Never produce one blended "CBGB" room. Minimum target states:

- `pre-cbgb-palace-bar` — inherited geometry/fixtures before 1973;
- `cbgb-1973-1976` — opening/proto-punk period;
- `cbgb-1977-1982` — punk/no-wave peak;
- `cbgb-1983-1989` — hardcore/1980s state;
- `cbgb-1990-1999`;
- `cbgb-2000-2005`;
- `cbgb-2006-closing`;
- `post-cbgb-2008-2009-preserved-retail`;
- `current-surviving-fabric`.

A geometry/object may persist across states only when evidence supports persistence.

## Spatial reconstruction method

### 1. Build the modern shell

Obtain permissioned current capture and measured anchors. Create a base mesh with real scale, walls, openings, ceiling planes and surviving fixed fabric.

### 2. Register archival viewpoints

For each historical image/video viewpoint, annotate fixed homologous points. Solve approximate camera pose against the shell. Reference-only media produces pose/observation records, not redistributed texture pixels.

### 3. Detect historical changes

For each time state, classify every visible feature as:

- `verified_persistent`;
- `verified_period_specific`;
- `probable`;
- `unknown`;
- `contradicted`.

### 4. Rebuild missing period features

Use owned/open/partner visuals for textures/models. Where only reference evidence exists, create a neutral researched approximation and label it as estimated until rights/geometry improve.

### 5. Score reconstruction confidence

A public object should carry:

- evidence IDs;
- valid date range;
- position confidence;
- dimension confidence;
- appearance confidence;
- rights confidence;
- reconstruction method.

## Rights-acquisition strategy

Do not try to license the whole Internet. Target sources that materially improve geometry or experience:

1. current 315 Bowery permissioned scan;
2. Kersavage/Reaven 2006 report;
3. selected NIGHTCLUBBING reels with room coverage rather than only tight performance shots;
4. one or two major photographer archives with overlapping wide interior views;
5. closing-era 2005-2006 wide shots;
6. selected landmark performances only after venue reconstruction is stable.

## POC build sequence

### Phase A — Evidence graph

- expand source registry;
- run open metadata collector;
- run Commons rights-aware asset collector;
- deduplicate assets by content hash and source identity;
- seed event timeline;
- record conflicts rather than resolving them silently.

### Phase B — Closing-era shell

- current survey or verified plans;
- solve entrance/bar/stage/rear anchors;
- register 2004-2009 open images;
- create `cbgb-2006-closing` geometry with confidence overlay.

### Phase C — Browser twin

- photoreal/open visual layer;
- simplified collision/navigation mesh;
- stage anchor and synthetic POC video;
- clickable evidence inspector;
- year selector;
- uncertainty/debug mode.

### Phase D — 1970s rollback

- use 1975 open Metropolis image and rights-cleared archival observations;
- register 1977-1980 NIGHTCLUBBING camera viewpoints;
- remove late-period objects not supported in the 1970s;
- model early signage/stage state separately.

### Phase E — Historical event mode

For each well-evidenced event, create a scene packet:

`event -> artists -> date -> historical room state -> poster/flyer metadata -> licensed/open visuals -> audio/video rights -> stage playback -> citations`.

## POC success criteria

The CBGB POC is successful when:

1. a user can enter a browser-walkable closing-era reconstruction;
2. entrance, bar, stage and rear anchors have evidence-backed positions;
3. the stage can display our generated/licensed test performance;
4. every visible historical feature can expose provenance and confidence;
5. the year/state selector demonstrably changes historically variable features;
6. copyrighted reference media never becomes an undocumented texture/training source;
7. the pipeline can repeat for another lost venue without custom one-off research logic.

## Non-goals for the first POC

- Claiming millimeter accuracy without a survey.
- Recreating every poster exactly before rights are cleared.
- Downloading copyrighted concert footage just because it is viewable online.
- Building all of Bowery before the single-room method works.
- Merging this research experiment into production/main before manual review.
